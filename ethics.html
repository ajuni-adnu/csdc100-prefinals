Responsible use of Generative AI in the academe

Human Input
AI is somewhat of a grey area in some academies so it’s important to hear out what policies they enforce, check if they allow the use of AI and which scenarios it is applicable on. According to the British School Manila, we can apply these 3 principles to use AI wisely: understand the technology, practice ethical use, and apply critical thinking.
AI Input

Avoiding plagiarism and misuse of Generative AI

Human Input
AI use depends on your professor but if they do allow it, to avoid plagiarism and misuse: it’s recommended to cite the AI as a source instead of claiming it as your own. Consider this APA format for parenthetical citation: (Source, Year). By doing so, you’re respecting intellectual property!
AI Input

Accuracy and verification of Generative AI outputs

Human Input
As of now, since AI lacks semantic understanding and doesn’t process the meaning of what it says: it’s possible to confidently state incorrect facts (Southern New Hampshire University). Like any other online apps, there’s no guarantee that the information it provides will be 100% accurate. It’s essential to watch out for AI hallucinations, question its legitimacy, and fact-check from multiple credible sources.
AI Input

Bias and fairness of Generative AI

Human Input
AI learns from data it is trained on, meaning it can also learn from biases (Southern New Hampshire University, n.d). Ensure that your information isn’t one-sided or lacks multiple viewpoints. It is advisable to rely on multiple credible sources to ground yourself the correct understanding of a particular situation.
